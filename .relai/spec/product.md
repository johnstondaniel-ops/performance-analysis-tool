# Product

## Target Users

### Primary — University Students
Undergraduate or postgraduate students enrolled in theatre, performance studies, dance, or music courses that require formal performance analysis. They have seen a live performance (theatre, dance, or music/opera) and need to document, organise, and interpret what they observed.

They are comfortable with digital tools but not necessarily with technical software. They need something that feels intuitive and doesn't get in the way of thinking.

### Secondary — Academics / Tutors
Course teachers who receive a shared map link from a student for review or feedback purposes. They are read-only users — they don't create maps themselves (at this stage). They may be the ones who introduce students to the tool as part of course materials.

## Core User Flows

### Flow 1 — Create a New Map (Stage 1: Material Signifiers)

1. Student arrives at the tool homepage
2. Clicks "New Map" — enters a performance title and optionally a date/venue
3. A multi-track canvas loads with default tracks based on McAuley's five categories:
   - **Stage / Space** (stage-auditorium relationship)
   - **Decor, Set & Lighting**
   - **Objects & Props**
   - **Actors — Appearance** (costume, hair, makeup, masks, vocal quality)
   - **Actors — Movement & Blocking** (utilisation of scenic space, gesture, juxtaposition)
   - **Music & Sound**
4. Student defines time markers along the horizontal axis (e.g. "Scene 1", "Act 2 opening", "Climax", or custom labels — no fixed format)
5. Student clicks into a track cell and types an observation (free text)
6. Student can add custom tracks (e.g. "Audience response", "Lighting subtext")
7. Student can hide tracks they don't need
8. Map is auto-saved and a unique share link is generated

### Flow 2 — Paradigm Sorting (Stage 2: Synchronic Analysis)

1. Student switches to "Paradigm View" from within their map
2. All observations from the map are displayed as individual cards
3. Student creates named paradigm groups (e.g. "Puppetry / mechanisation", "Horizontal vs vertical tension")
4. Student drags observations into paradigm groups
5. Paradigm view can be exported or shared alongside the map

### Flow 3 — Export & Share

1. From either view, student clicks "Export" — downloads as PDF or image
2. Student clicks "Share" — copies a unique read-only URL to send to their tutor

## Design Direction

The tool should feel **creative and expressive** — it's a tool for analysing art, and it should reflect that. Not cold, not corporate, not spreadsheet-like.

Inspiration: the visual language of arts programmes, theatre posters, music notation software. A dark or rich colour palette would suit the subject matter. Typography should feel considered — perhaps serif headings with clean sans-serif UI text.

The multi-track canvas (Stage 1) can lean into the DAW metaphor visually — horizontal tracks, clear time markers, a sense of performance unfolding left to right.

The paradigm sorting view (Stage 2) should feel more like an ideas board — cards, groupings, a sense of clustering and discovery.

No strong brand guidelines exist yet. Colour palette and logo are TBD.

## Performance Types Covered

- Theatre / drama (including text-based, devised, and avant-garde)
- Dance (ballet, contemporary, physical theatre)
- Music / opera

McAuley's schema is most naturally suited to theatrical performance but the tool should accommodate all three, with track names and prompts flexible enough to serve each.

## Business Model

This is not a commercial product. It is being built for a **specific university course or institution**. No monetisation, subscriptions, or payments are required.

Distribution will likely be via course materials — a URL shared with students by their tutor.
